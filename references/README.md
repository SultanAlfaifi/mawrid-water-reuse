# References

This library separates regulatory context, technical guidance, and peer-reviewed evidence. A reference supports only the claim stated beside it; none of these sources certifies MAWRID.

## Ablution greywater

1. **Ameer, S., Muhammad, N., Jadoon, K. Z., Sheikh, N., & Rahman, K. U. (2025).** “A systematic literature review on potential of ablution greywater reuse: current insights, challenges, and future prospects.” *Applied Water Science*, 15, 173. [https://doi.org/10.1007/s13201-025-02488-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13201-025-02488-0)
   - Supports: reuse potential, source variability, treatment literature, and continuing technical, regulatory, and adoption gaps.
   - Boundary: a review does not validate MAWRID or transfer every reported result to a Saudi pilot.

2. **Hassan, R., Alluqmani, A. E., & Badawi, A. K. (2024).** “An eco-friendly solution for greywater treatment via date palm fiber filter.” *Desalination and Water Treatment*, 317, 100163. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dwt.2024.100163](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dwt.2024.100163)
   - Supports: date-palm fibre was tested at pilot scale using water sampled from 24 Riyadh mosques.
   - Boundary: date-palm fibre is not biochar; the study does not establish MAWRID microbial safety, code compliance, or full-scale durability.

3. **O’Toole, J. et al. (2012).** “Microbial quality assessment of household greywater.” *Water Research*, 46. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2012.05.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2012.05.001)
   - Supports: greywater can contain microbial hazards and requires risk-based management.
   - Boundary: household greywater is not identical to source-separated ablution water.

## Date-palm biochar and adsorption

4. **Abdelaal, A., Ahmed, A., Wang, H., Dalahmeh, S. S., Chehimi, M. M., Abdullah, A. M., & Jlassi, K. (2026).** “Date palm waste biochar for the adsorptive removal of pharmaceuticals: a review.” *Environmental Sciences Europe*, 38, 59. [https://doi.org/10.1186/s12302-026-01333-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12302-026-01333-8)
   - Supports: date-palm waste is a viable biochar precursor and manufacturing conditions affect adsorption properties.
   - Boundary: this is not an ablution-greywater field trial or evidence of pathogen removal.

5. **Kearns, J. P. et al. (2021).** “Biochar Water Treatment for Control of Organic Micropollutants with UVA Surrogate Monitoring.” *Environmental Engineering Science*, 38(5), 298–309. [https://doi.org/10.1089/ees.2020.0173](https://doi.org/10.1089/ees.2020.0173)
   - Supports: UVA254 may act as an economical surrogate for adsorption-bed condition under tested circumstances.
   - Boundary: UVA254 is not a pathogen indicator, universal contaminant detector, or substitute for laboratory validation.

6. **Kundu, S. et al. (2025).** “Realistic and field scale applications of biochar for water remediation: A literature review.” *Journal of Environmental Management*. [Open-access full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12160065/)
   - Supports: biochar has field and pilot potential, with continuing scale-up and performance uncertainties.
   - Boundary: biochar does not consistently outperform commercial activated carbon.

7. **Lawal, A. A. et al. (2022).** “A comprehensive review on the chemical regeneration of biochar adsorbent for sustainable wastewater treatment.” *npj Clean Water*. [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41545-022-00172-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41545-022-00172-3)
   - Supports: regeneration may extend media life but creates performance and secondary-waste considerations.

## Risk, reuse, and test frameworks

8. **World Health Organization.** *Guidelines for the Safe Use of Wastewater, Excreta and Greywater, Volume 4.* [WHO publication page](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9241546859)
   - Supports: risk-based health targets and management for greywater use in agriculture.
   - Boundary: it is not a Saudi permit or an indoor toilet-flushing certification.

9. **US Environmental Protection Agency (2025).** *Risk-Based Framework for Developing Microbial Treatment Targets for Water Reuse.* [Official PDF](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-01/risk-basedframeworkfordevelopingmicrobialtreatmenttargetsforwaterreuse-1.pdf)
   - Supports: source-and-use-specific microbial risk assessment and treatment-target development.

10. **US Environmental Protection Agency.** [Onsite Non-Potable Water Reuse Resources](https://www.epa.gov/waterreuse/onsite-non-potable-water-reuse-resources)
    - Supports: fit-for-purpose onsite reuse design resources and risk-based treatment context.

11. **NSF/ANSI 350-2026.** *Onsite Residential and Commercial Water Reuse Treatment Systems.* [ANSI catalog record](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/nsf/nsfansi3502026)
    - Supports: an international product-performance benchmark for onsite reuse systems.
    - Boundary: the standard is paid, and MAWRID is not certified or claimed to comply.

12. **APHA, AWWA, & WEF.** *Standard Methods for the Examination of Water and Wastewater*, 24th edition. [Publisher page](https://secure.apha.org/imis/ItemDetail?CATEGORY=BK&iProductCode=978-087553-2998)
    - Supports: recognized analytical methods for physical, chemical, and microbiological water testing.

## Citation policy

- Prefer DOI or official primary-source URLs.
- Record the accessed version and date for regulatory documents.
- Do not upload copyrighted papers without permission.
- Attach a citation to every quantitative literature claim.
- Never present a literature value as a MAWRID result.
- Archive pilot methods, raw data, detection limits, and calibration records with every published result.
